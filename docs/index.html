Ranking Webpages for Content Review Using Search Performance Signals

Abdelrahman Shaheen — FlyRank ML Internship Capstone

Abstract

This study asks whether search-performance signals observed during March 2026 can help rank webpages that are more likely to experience a meaningful impressions decline in April 2026. A page-level dataset was constructed using five March-only features: impressions, clicks, click-through rate, average search position, and active days. A transparent CTR-based baseline, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest were evaluated on the same client-grouped holdout with zero client overlap between training and testing. Logistic Regression produced the strongest measured ranking, achieving Precision@20 of 0.850 and Precision@50 of 0.700, compared with 0.500 and 0.540 for the deterministic baseline. The resulting scores were converted into a human-reviewed content action playbook intended to prioritize investigation rather than automate editorial decisions.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content and SEO teams may manage far more webpages than they can manually inspect at once. The practical question is therefore not only whether a page may decline, but which pages should be reviewed first.

This project investigates whether a small set of measured search-performance signals can create a useful ranking of pages for human review.

The research question is: Can search-performance signals observed during March 2026 help rank webpages that are more likely to experience a meaningful impressions decline in April 2026?

The system is designed as a ranking and decision-support tool. It does not attempt to predict Google's algorithm, prove why a page declined, or automatically change content.

2. Data

This project uses the protected FlyRank ML Internship warehouse dataset and focuses on the daily content-performance data.

The feature window is March 1–31, 2026. Only information available by the end of March is used as model input. The outcome window is April 1–30, 2026, and April data is used only to construct the future decline label and evaluate the ranking.

The final analysis contained:

  • 101,441 page-level rows
  • 44 pseudonymized clients
  • 51.7% observed next-month decline rate

Each row represents one pseudonymized webpage or content item for one client.

Model features

  • march_impressions
  • march_clicks
  • march_ctr_pct
  • march_avg_position
  • march_active_days

Pages were included only when they had at least 100 March impressions and were observed during April. The future label is_next_month_decline equals 1 when April impressions are more than 20% below March impressions.

April measurements, the outcome label itself, private client names, real URLs, and private search queries were excluded from model inputs. Client and content identifiers were used only for grouping, joining, reproducibility, and validation.

3. Methodology

The task is treated primarily as a ranking problem. Each webpage receives a score and pages with higher scores are reviewed first.

Three approaches were evaluated:

  • A deterministic CTR-based baseline
  • Logistic Regression
  • Random Forest

The dataset was split by client_hash_id using a grouped holdout. This produced 93,085 training rows and 8,356 testing rows, with zero client overlap between the two groups.

The main evaluation metric is Precision@50, with Precision@20 also reported. Precision@K measures how many pages among the top K ranked recommendations actually experienced the defined April decline.

A leakage audit confirmed that all model features are available by the end of March. April impressions and the target label are never used as predictive inputs.

4. Results

Key result: Logistic Regression produced the strongest measured ranking on the grouped-client holdout, reaching Precision@20 = 0.850 and Precision@50 = 0.700.
Method Precision@20 Precision@50
Deterministic baseline 0.500 0.540
Logistic Regression 0.850 0.700
Random Forest 0.750 0.580

At Precision@50, Logistic Regression identified 35 declining pages among its top 50 recommendations. The deterministic baseline identified 27 of 50, while Random Forest identified 29 of 50.

At Precision@20, Logistic Regression identified 17 declining pages among the top 20, compared with 10 for the baseline and 15 for Random Forest.

These results do not show that Logistic Regression is universally superior. They show that, for this March-to-April evaluation, five-feature design, and grouped-client holdout, Logistic Regression produced the strongest measured ranking among the tested approaches.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

This is a directional, decision-support analysis rather than a causal study.

The model was evaluated on one specific time transition: March 2026 features and an April 2026 outcome. The measured performance should not be assumed to transfer unchanged to all future months, clients, topics, or search environments.

The decline label is also a practical proxy. A page is labeled as declining when April impressions are more than 20% below March impressions, but that does not prove the page has a permanent content problem.

The model does not directly observe many potentially important factors, including:

  • changes in search demand
  • competitor activity
  • SERP layout changes
  • seasonality
  • tracking changes
  • site migrations or technical changes
  • content quality or factual accuracy
  • content age or freshness
  • business importance
  • user intent

Therefore, a high score should not be interpreted as proof that a page is stale, low quality, or necessarily in need of rewriting.

The analysis identifies measured associations and ranking performance; it does not establish that changing CTR, ranking position, content length, freshness, or another feature would cause future performance to improve.

6. Ranked Recommendations

Logistic Regression scores are converted into a human-reviewed content action playbook.

The high-priority threshold is the top 10% of model scores, approximately 0.593. The elevated-priority threshold begins at the 60th percentile, approximately 0.496.

Action Reason code Pages

REVIEW_CONTENT_AND_RANKING HIGH_SCORE_OTHER_SIGNALS 55
MONITOR_AND_REVIEW ELEVATED_DECLINE_SCORE 2,507
MONITOR LOWER_DECLINE_SCORE 5,013

Action distribution

These actions are recommendations for review, not diagnoses. For example, REVIEW_CTR_AND_REFRESH does not mean the model detected stale content. The model contains no direct freshness or content-age feature.

A human reviewer should inspect search intent, title and metadata, page quality, freshness, tracking context, business importance, seasonality, and site changes before deciding whether any action is appropriate.

The intended workflow is:

model ranking → reason code → human review → human decision → measured follow-up

The system should not automatically rewrite, publish, delete, redirect, deindex, or otherwise modify pages from model scores alone.
